# IRB — Data Generation Method Statement

Short description of the synthetic-image data-generation method, for inclusion in
the IRB application. Covers: (1) images are artificial, generated to simulate
specific facial conditions; (2) not based on Mayo Clinic patients and contain no
identifiable patient data; (3) wholly synthetic → proposed exempt from HIPAA
disclosure, requiring only verbal observer consent.

## Recommended (methodology-emphasized, ~4 sentences)

> The facial images used in this study are artificial images produced by prompting a
> commercially available, pre-trained text-to-image generative model (OpenAI GPT
> Image, model "gpt-image-2") with structured clinical text descriptions; no patient
> images, photographs, or other source images are used as input to the model. Each
> prompt is assembled programmatically from (i) a fixed clinical-photography style
> descriptor (a frontal, oblique, or profile view of the face in a clinical setting),
> (ii) a clinically specific description of the target facial defect and its state —
> either a long-standing/congenital pre-reconstruction presentation or a well-healed
> post-reconstruction result — and (iii) randomized, non-identifying attributes (age
> range, sex, ethnicity, and clinical background) so that the resulting set is diverse
> across the conditions of interest (e.g., facial paralysis/paresis, post-Mohs and
> head-and-neck oncologic defects, cleft lip, and post-traumatic deformity). These
> images are entirely synthetic: they are not derived from, and do not depict, any
> Mayo Clinic patient or any real, identifiable individual, and they contain no
> protected health information (PHI); the exact prompt, model version, and generation
> parameters for every image are recorded to ensure full reproducibility. Because the
> images are wholly computer-generated and contain no PHI, we believe they are exempt
> from the HIPAA requirements governing disclosure of patient information, and that
> only verbal consent from study observers is required for their use.

## Standalone Methodology paragraph (drop-in)

> Synthetic facial images were generated using a commercially available, pre-trained
> text-to-image generative model (OpenAI GPT Image, model "gpt-image-2"). The model
> was conditioned solely on natural-language clinical prompts; no real photographs,
> patient records, or other images were provided as input. For each target condition,
> a prompt was composed from a standardized clinical-photography style template, a
> clinically specific description of the defect and its stage (long-standing/
> pre-reconstruction vs. well-healed post-reconstruction), and randomized
> non-identifying demographic and clinical-setting descriptors to ensure visual
> diversity. Images were generated at high resolution; prompts that triggered the
> provider's automated content-safety filter were automatically re-submitted. The
> prompt text, model identifier, and all generation parameters were logged for every
> image, making the entire dataset fully reproducible.

## Notes / supporting facts (for reviewer questions)

- Inputs are **text prompts only** — no patient images, records, or other data are
  used as input to the model, which reinforces that no PHI is involved.
- The model does not reproduce specific real individuals; outputs are novel,
  non-identifiable synthetic faces.
- Full reproducibility: every image's prompt, parameters, and model version are
  logged (`metadata/generations.jsonl`).
- The final HIPAA/exemption determination rests with the IRB; the language above is
  phrased as the study team's position ("we believe…"), which is the conventional
  framing for that determination.
